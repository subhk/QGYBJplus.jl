QG equations

Generalized potential vorticity

The prognostic field is the total generalized PV,

\[q = q^g + q^w,\]

with balanced component

\[q^g = \nabla_h^2\psi + \partial_z\!\left(\frac{f_0^2}{N^2(z)}\partial_z\psi\right).\]

For constant stratification this becomes

\[q^g = \nabla_h^2\psi + \frac{f_0^2}{N^2}\partial_z^2\psi.\]

When the flow evolves,

\[\partial_t q + J(\psi,q) = \mathcal D_q, \qquad J(a,b)=\partial_xa\,\partial_yb-\partial_ya\,\partial_xb.\]

$\mathcal D_q$ denotes the configured horizontal and vertical dissipation. A FixedFlow() model holds the balanced flow fixed instead of advancing this equation.

Wave contribution

With WaveMeanFeedback(), the dimensional wave envelope contributes

\[q^w = \frac{i}{2f_0}J(B^*,B) + \frac{1}{4f_0}\nabla_h^2|B|^2.\]

The factors of $1/f_0$ are part of the dimensional equation. With NoFeedback() or NoWaveFeedback(), the solver uses $q^w=0$.

The prognostic $q$ is then the total PV, but the flow's dissipation $\mathcal D_q$ acts on the balanced PV $q-q^w$ only: $q^w$ belongs to the waves, which have their own damping. Damping the total PV instead would create a balanced flow from the wave PV and could add energy. The time stepper keeps the exact damping factor on $q$ and adds the damping of $q^w$ to the explicit part, so modes whose damping is very stiff relax to $q = q^w$.

For YBJPlus(), this is the wave part of the "XV$^+$" potential vorticity of Asselin & Young (2019), equation (3.5), with $B=L^+A$. For the original YBJ() formulation the prognostic envelope is instead $B=LA$, recovering the original Xie–Vanneste PV. A&Y reach (3.5) from that PV by the substitution $L\mapsto L^+$, and that substitution is what gives the YBJ^+` coupled system its "coupled energy" conservation law (their equations (3.6)–(3.7)).

Note the intended contrast with wave kinetic energy, which uses $LA$: A&Y equation (4.7) and the remark that "to define WKE for YBJ$^+$ we use $L$, not $L^+$".

Streamfunction inversion

The streamfunction is diagnosed from balanced PV:

\[\nabla_h^2\psi + \partial_z\!\left(\frac{f_0^2}{N^2}\partial_z\psi\right) = q-q^w.\]

Wave PV is subtracted only on the right-hand side of the inversion; the prognostic total q itself is never modified.

After horizontal Fourier transformation, every $(k_x,k_y)$ mode is a tridiagonal vertical problem,

\[-k_h^2\widehat\psi + \partial_z\!\left(a(z)\partial_z\widehat\psi\right) = \widehat{q-q^w}, \qquad a(z)=\frac{f_0^2}{N^2(z)}.\]

The public inversion entry point is:

invert_q_to_psi!(model)

Velocity

Balanced horizontal velocity and relative vorticity follow from

\[u=-\partial_y\psi, \qquad v=\partial_x\psi, \qquad \zeta=\nabla_h^2\psi.\]

Use model-level operators so transforms, work arrays, and the architecture are selected from the owning runtime:

compute_velocities!(model)
compute_vertical_velocity!(model)

Horizontal derivatives and Jacobians are pseudo-spectral and use two-thirds dealiasing. Vertical operators use second-order differences. See numerical methods.

Vertical velocity

The balanced flow carries an ageostrophic vertical velocity. Eliminating the time derivatives between the vorticity and buoyancy equations,

\[\partial_t\zeta + J(\psi,\zeta) = f_0\,\partial_z w, \qquad \partial_t b + J(\psi,b) + N^2 w = 0, \qquad b = f_0\,\partial_z\psi,\]

gives the omega equation,

\[N^2\nabla_h^2 w + f_0^2\,\partial_z^2 w = 2\nabla\cdot\mathbf Q = 2f_0\left[J(\psi_z,\zeta) - J(\psi_x,\psi_{xz}) - J(\psi_y,\psi_{yz})\right],\]

with $w=0$ at the bottom and the rigid lid. compute_vertical_velocity! solves it on the faces between levels, where the model's buoyancy $f_0\,\delta_z\psi$ lives, with $N^2$ sampled on the faces. The discrete forcing is the one implied by the flux-form PV inversion, so the diagnosed $w$ satisfies the model's own discrete vorticity and buoyancy equations exactly.

With wave feedback the conserved PV is $q^g+q^w$, so the vorticity equation gains $-D_t q^w$ and the omega equation the forcing $f_0\,\partial_z(D_t q^w)$, where $D_t q^w = \partial_t q^w + J(\psi,q^w)$ and $\partial_t q^w$ follows from the inviscid wave tendency. The $w$ of a coupled run then closes the coupled buoyancy equation exactly. As $\psi$ is the Lagrangian-mean streamfunction (Xie & Vanneste 2015; Wagner & Young 2016), $u$, $v$, and $w$ are Lagrangian-mean velocities; see Wave–mean interaction for the instantaneous velocity.

The vertical velocity is a diagnostic: QG advection of $q$ is horizontal, so $w$ does not feed back on the flow. It is computed on demand, by compute_velocities!(model), compute_vertical_velocity!(model), and NetCDF output with velocities; a time step leaves w = 0. Particles move horizontally and do not use it.

The face values are averaged onto the cell centres for storage, so u, v, and w share one grid in output files. The wave-induced vertical velocity of compute_ybj_vertical_velocity! is stored the same way (see YBJ⁺ wave model).
